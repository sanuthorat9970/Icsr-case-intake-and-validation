Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR): Case Intake and Validation

1. Introduction

An Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) is a structured report containing information about a suspected adverse event or adverse drug reaction (ADR) in an individual patient. ICSRs are important in pharmacovigilance because they support the collection, assessment, and monitoring of medicine safety information.

ICSR case intake and validation involve receiving a safety report, checking its required information, identifying missing details, and determining whether follow-up is needed.

This project is an educational demonstration using fictional data.

2. Aim

To demonstrate a systematic process for receiving, documenting, and validating an ICSR while identifying incomplete or inconsistent information.

3. Objectives

- Understand the basic ICSR intake process.
- Identify the minimum information needed for a valid ICSR.
- Check case information for completeness and consistency.
- Identify missing data and prepare follow-up questions.
- Assign an appropriate documentation status.
- Maintain confidentiality and accurate records.

4. Scope

This project covers basic ICSR intake and validation using a fictional adverse drug reaction case. It demonstrates data collection, completeness checks, discrepancy identification, and follow-up documentation.

It is not a validated pharmacovigilance database or a substitute for official reporting procedures.

5. Required Information for a Valid ICSR

The commonly recognized minimum criteria for a valid ICSR are:

1. Identifiable patient: Sufficient information to distinguish the patient, according to applicable reporting rules.
2. Identifiable reporter: Information establishing that the report came from a reporter.
3. Suspect medicinal product: The medicine suspected of being associated with the event.
4. Suspected adverse reaction or event: A description of the reported medical occurrence.

Additional information, such as dates, dose, route, medical history, treatment, outcome, and concomitant medicines, may be needed for a complete assessment.

6. Procedure

Step 1: Receive the Report

Receive the report through an appropriate reporting channel and record the date of receipt.

Step 2: Create a Case Identifier

Assign a unique internal case number to support tracking and avoid duplicate records.

Step 3: Collect Case Information

Record the available patient, reporter, suspected medicine, and adverse event information.

Step 4: Check Minimum Validity Criteria

Verify whether the report contains all four minimum criteria for a valid ICSR. If information is missing, document the gap and follow the applicable procedure.

Step 5: Validate Data

Check for missing fields, inconsistent dates, unclear medicine details, duplicate reports, and contradictory information.

Step 6: Document Missing Information

Prepare follow-up questions for the reporter when necessary. Do not invent missing information.

Step 7: Record the Validation Result

Document whether the case meets the minimum validity criteria, requires follow-up, or needs further review.

Step 8: Maintain Confidentiality

Use only authorized data and protect patient and reporter information according to applicable privacy requirements.

Step 9: Final Review

Have the case reviewed by an appropriately trained pharmacovigilance professional before any real-world regulatory submission.

10. Validation Outcome

Educational status: Incomplete information; follow-up required.

The fictional case includes a suspected medicine and a reported event. However, the patient and reporter are represented by training placeholders, so this example does not establish that the report meets all minimum criteria for a valid ICSR.

In an actual case, validity must be determined using the applicable pharmacovigilance rules and the real source information. Missing dates or dose details do not automatically make every report invalid if the minimum validity criteria are otherwise met.

11. Follow-Up Questions

The following questions may be considered where appropriate:

1. What was the date the patient started taking the medicine?
2. When did the skin rash and itching begin?
3. What dose and route were used?
4. Was any treatment given for the reaction?
5. What was the patient's outcome?
6. Were any other medicines being taken?
7. Is additional relevant medical history available?

Only request information relevant to the case and applicable reporting requirements.

12. Common ICSR Validation Errors

- Missing minimum validity information.
- Incomplete medicine or event descriptions.
- Incorrect or inconsistent dates.
- Duplicate case records.
- Unclear reporter or patient information.
- Unsupported conclusions about causality.
- Failure to document follow-up attempts.
- Exposure of confidential patient data.

13. Recommendations

- Use a standardized intake form.
- Apply the current applicable ICSR reporting guidance.
- Record missing information clearly.
- Check for duplicate cases.
- Maintain an audit trail of updates.
- Protect patient and reporter confidentiality.
- Escalate uncertain cases for qualified review.
- Use approved systems and procedures for regulatory reporting.

14. Conclusion

ICSR case intake and validation are important steps in pharmacovigilance. A structured workflow helps identify minimum validity information, detect data gaps, document follow-up needs, and support reliable safety reporting. Accurate documentation, confidentiality, and compliance with applicable reporting requirements are essential.
